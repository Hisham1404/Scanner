How to actually hold it

  1. Kill the room lights and draw the curtains. The scanner works on the difference the torch makes, so the darker the room, the larger that difference is.
  2. Hold the phone at arm’s length, screen towards you. The torch has to be beside the lens you are looking through — that is the whole trick. Watch the screen, not the room.
  3. Go slowly. A candidate has to be seen across several detection cycles before it scores highly, so sweeping fast defeats the thing that makes this reliable. Count about three seconds per metre of wall.
  4. Stand roughly one to three metres back. Too close and the torch beam overshoots the spot; too far and a lens is too few pixels to resolve. Use zoom rather than walking closer.
  5. Cross every suspect spot twice, from different angles. A chrome tap or a glossy tile throws a highlight that slides away and dies when you move. A lens keeps returning light to you. That difference is the real test, and it is yours to make, not the app’s.
  6. Sweep at the heights that matter: ceiling, then above eye level, then waist height. Do the ceiling as its own pass with nothing else on your mind.

Reading the result

70% and up — something is bouncing light straight back at you. Go and look at that spot with your hands.

48–69% — a reflector of some kind. Could be a lens, could be chrome, glass or a polished screw head. Re-cross it in Pulse mode from a different angle.

Below 48% — ordinary shiny surface. Keep sweeping.

What this will flag that is not a camera Polished taps and shower fittings, mirror edges, glass and spectacle lenses, screw heads, smooth tile, TV and phone screens, glossy paint, foil, cat and dog eyes, and reflective thread in curtains. Retroreflection is a property of a lens, not a property unique to cameras. The persistence test above is what separates them — do it every time.
Do not go straight to confrontation If you find something in someone else’s property, the next page you want is Legal, not the reception desk. There is a right order for this, and getting it wrong destroys the evidence.
