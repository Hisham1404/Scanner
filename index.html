Read this first

A clean sweep means you did not find a camera. It does not mean there isn’t one. Cameras that record to an SD card and transmit nothing are invisible to every app, every detector and every network scan on the market. Optics and your hands are the only things that reach those.

Method
01

Lens-glint scan

A lens focused onto a sensor bounces light straight back at its source. Your torch sits beside your camera, so a hidden lens lights up as a small bright dot. The scanner strobes the torch and reads only the light the torch put there, which cancels out room lights, standby LEDs and windows.

What works, and what does not

Most detector apps are not honest about this, so here it is plainly.

MethodVerdictWhy
Lens retroreflection Works The one method that reaches every device type, including offline SD-card cameras. Published work measured 88.9% detection this way against 46% for the naked eye.
Physical search Works Unglamorous and effective. In nearly every reported case, a person found the camera by noticing something out of place.
Infrared LED spotting Barely Night-vision cameras do emit infrared, but flagship phone cameras filter it out. Measured detection on current Samsung and Apple flagships is under 10%.
Magnetometer apps No Deliberately excluded. One published test had a magnetometer app flag 47 anomalies in a single hotel room, none of which were cameras.
Network scanning Not here The strongest automated signal for networked cameras, and impossible from a browser on any platform. Needs a native app — which is the main reason to build one.
On your phone as an instrument

A bright torch mounted beside a high-resolution camera with optical zoom is genuinely the right hardware for retroreflection work. What it is not is a radio scanner. That part needs the app.

Scanner runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you scan, flag or write is uploaded — there is no server, no account, no analytics. Findings live in this browser’s local storage and clearing site data erases them.
