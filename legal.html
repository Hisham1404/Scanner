If you find one

The first five minutes decide the case.

The instinct is to rip it out. That instinct destroys the two things that convict — the device in position, and the footage on it.

Do not do these
  • Do not unplug it, switch it off, or pull out the memory card.
  • Do not pocket it or move it to another room.
  • Do not wipe it, and try not to add your fingerprints to it.
  • Do not tell the hotel, landlord, warden or shop staff first. If it is their device, the warning gives them time to remove it — and it will be gone before anyone official arrives.
  • Do not post it online before you report it.

Do these, in this order

  1. Get yourself somewhere it cannot see you. Step out of frame first. Everything else can wait ninety seconds.
  2. Photograph it exactly where it is. A wide shot showing which room and which wall, a medium shot showing what it is mounted on, and a close-up of the device. Record a slow video walking from the door to the device so the position is unambiguous. Keep your phone’s timestamps on.
  3. Cover the lens without moving the device. A towel, tape or a bag over it. This stops further recording while leaving the scene intact.
  4. Call the police. 112 for emergency. If you are in Kerala, the Kerala Police control room and the district cyber cell both take this seriously — there is a record of arrests in these cases.
  5. File online as well. cybercrime.gov.in is the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal and has a dedicated women-and-children stream that allows anonymous reporting.
  6. Write it down while it is fresh. The Report tab builds a timestamped account and a complaint draft from whatever you flagged.
  7. Insist on a written acknowledgement. Ask for the FIR number or the portal acknowledgement number and keep it.
  8. Stay out of the room if you can. If you are in a hotel, ask for a different room in a different part of the building, or leave. Do not accept an offer to “have someone come and check it”.
If you think footage is already online Report the specific URLs through cybercrime.gov.in, and use the platform’s own non-consensual intimate imagery reporting route at the same time. Under the IT Rules, intermediaries are required to act on such complaints within a short window. Save the URLs and screenshots before reporting — the content may come down and you will still need to show what was there.

Who to contact

112 — national emergency number. Use it if the person may still be present or you feel unsafe.

cybercrime.gov.in — National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal. Complaints about recording, sharing or publishing images go here, including anonymously.

1091 — women’s helpline.

1930 — cybercrime helpline.

Local police station and district cyber cell — go in person and file a written complaint as well as reporting online. The online portal alone does not always produce an FIR.

The law that applies

Enough to know your footing. For anything beyond that, talk to a lawyer — this is not legal advice.

Section 77, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 — voyeurism

Covers watching or capturing the image of a woman engaged in a private act where she would expect not to be observed, and covers passing those images on.

Punishment: first conviction, one to three years and a fine. Subsequent conviction, three to seven years and a fine.

Section 66E, Information Technology Act, 2000 — violation of privacy

Covers intentionally capturing, publishing or transmitting an image of the private area of any person without consent, in circumstances violating their privacy. Unlike Section 77, this one is not limited to women.

Punishment: up to three years, or a fine up to two lakh rupees, or both.

Both usually apply together In the reported Kerala cases, arrests were made under the IT Act and the criminal law together. Depending on facts there may be more — POCSO where a minor is involved, and additional provisions where the footage was shared or sold. Say what happened and let the police and a lawyer decide the sections; do not talk yourself out of reporting because you are unsure which one fits.

If it is your own home or rental

  • A landlord installing a camera inside a let property, in a private area, without your knowledge, is not a grey area — report it the same way.
  • Photograph the device in place before you touch it, exactly as above, then report it before confronting anyone.
  • Keep your rental agreement, payment records and the listing. They establish that the space was yours and that you had an expectation of privacy in it.
  • Cameras in shared or common areas that are openly disclosed are a different matter from a concealed camera in a bathroom or bedroom. Concealment and the private area are what make it an offence.

If you are a woman reporting this in India

  • You can have your statement recorded by a woman officer, and at your residence rather than at the station, if you prefer.
  • You may take a support person with you. You do not have to go alone.
  • A police station cannot refuse to register a cognisable offence. If one does, escalate to the Superintendent of Police, and file at cybercrime.gov.in in parallel so there is a dated record.
  • Do not let anyone talk you into a private settlement. In several of the reported cases, the accused was an employee, a colleague or a person in authority at the place, and pressure to keep it quiet came from that direction.
