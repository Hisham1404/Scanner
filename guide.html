The row that matters is local storage

A camera recording to an SD card transmits nothing. No network scan, no RF detector and no app will ever see it. In the Delhi rented-home case the landlord’s son simply pulled the memory cards by hand and copied the footage to a laptop. Only optics and a physical search reach these, which is why the scanner and the sweep come before anything else here.

Reported cases

What the device actually turned out to be, and how it surfaced. That last part is the useful one — in nearly every case a person noticed something out of place, not a gadget.

What gives them away
01

Power

A camera runs on mains or a battery, and battery cameras die in hours. Anything meant to run for a shift is plugged in. Count the things plugged in and find the one nobody can explain.

02

A hole with no purpose

One to three millimetres across, on a face with no grille, no screw and no LED. Fresh drill dust beneath it, fresh filler around it, or a bead of silicone.

03

Line of sight

Every camera must see the thing it was put there to see. Stand where you would undress and look back at every object that can see you. That narrows a room to a handful of objects very quickly.

04

Heat

Continuous recording makes a device warm. Touch the back of anything you are unsure about.

05

Objects out of context

A charger in a bathroom. A clock facing the bed instead of the door. A dustbin with wiring. A smoke detector in a room with no others. This is what actually catches them.

06

Newness

One bright screw among tarnished ones, disturbed paint, a switchboard cleaner than the wall around it.

Why most detector apps do not work

Worth knowing, because you will be offered plenty of them.

Magnetometer apps

They read the phone’s compass and call any disturbance a camera. A published test had one flag 47 separate anomalies in a single hotel room, none of which were cameras. Wiring in walls, pipes, hinges, door frames and speakers all set it off. This tool deliberately has no magnetometer mode.

Infrared modes

Real physics — night-vision cameras do emit infrared at 850 or 940 nm — but modern phone cameras have strong infrared-cut filters over the sensor. Measured detection rates on current Samsung and Apple flagships fall below 10%. It is included here as a third mode with that caveat attached, not as a test to rely on.

What is left

Lens retroreflection, which has published support behind it, and network scanning, which works well but needs a native app. Everything else being sold is noise.
